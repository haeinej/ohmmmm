# The home's bridge: `ohmmmm serve`

## For people

The app at app.ohmmmm.com runs in your browser, and a web page cannot open the folders on your computer. Your home folder is where your originals live, images included, and where your agents' memory (the cortex) is kept. `ohmmmm serve` is the bridge between the two: a small server on your computer that lets the app, and agents on the same computer, reach your home.

Start it inside your home:

```sh
cd ~/ohmmmm.alice.example.com
npx github:haeinej/ohmmmm serve
```

The first time, it prints a link. Open it once in the browser you use the app in, and the app is paired with your home. The link carries that browser's key in the part of the address after `#`, which browsers never send to any server. The app keeps the key in that browser only and takes it out of the address bar straight away. Your home keeps no copy of it, so the link is shown only once: to pair another browser, or this one again, run `ohmmmm pair` in your home, even while the bridge is running. Chrome may ask whether the app may reach devices on your local network; say yes.

What it does today:

- **Your home in the app:** once paired, the app opens on your own home instead of the sample. Its tree, graph, search and editor show your vault's files. What you write is saved into those files a moment later, recorded as yours, with the version before kept. Properties the app doesn't use stay as they were, in their place. If a file changed on disk since the app read it, the app does not write over it: it tells you, and you open your home again to see the change. If you try to leave while a change is still on its way, the browser asks first. Settings › Vault says which home you are looking at, and **Use the sample** goes back to the sample.
- **Moving and renaming:** moving a block to another channel, or renaming or moving a channel, renames it in your home the way `ohmmmm vault rename` does: the files move, and every link that used the old path is rewritten.
- **History:** the app's history starts from your vault's own journal: what was made and changed, by you, by an agent, or outside ohm (such as in Obsidian), with the versions the vault kept. When the app opens, it first looks for changes made since your vault was last indexed.
- **Who can see a block:** the visibility control moves a block between Only you and Friends, in its file, after you approve. A Friends block is marked "not shared yet": it stays on your computer until your home syncs with the vault service. An agent never changes who sees a block itself. It asks, with a reason, and the app shows you each request to approve or decline.
- **Publishing:** choosing **Everyone** on a block shows what publishing it would do: the address it gets on your site, its label (your channel's name the first time, which you can change; it is then kept on the channel for next time), the images that go with it, and links that become plain words because what they point to is not published. Nothing is written until you press Publish. Then the bridge signs in to your PDS with your app password, writes the post, and marks the block's file public. If the answer is lost on the way, or something fails after the post is written, pressing Publish again finishes that same post: the block's ID is saved in its file before anything is sent, so it never becomes a second post. **Everyone** on a public block updates its post, which keeps its address and date. Choosing Only you or Friends on a public block takes its post down first, then moves the block; copies others already made are not recalled. Agents cannot publish or take a post down.
- **Not here yet:** syncing with the vault service, and your cortex.
- **Images:** when the app shows an image this browser does not have, it fetches the image from your home folder by its content address. Images you add go into your home folder as media blocks, each image file with its block beside it, recorded as yours.
- **Agents:** an agent on your computer can use your vault's tools at `/vault/mcp` and your cortex's at `/cortex/mcp`, with a **key of its own**. Make one with `ohmmmm agents add claude-code` (any name you like), and give that agent the key it prints, never the app's link. The key opens only those tools. Every session it opens is that agent, whatever the agent calls itself, so your grants for that name decide what it may change (`ohm vault grant claude-code …`) and the journal names it truly. It cannot read or write your files directly, answer requests, or publish. `ohmmmm agents` lists who has a key; `ohmmmm agents remove <name>` takes one away; `ohmmmm agents add <name> --new-key` replaces a lost one.

To publish, the bridge needs two things on your computer:

- Your site's configuration, as `ohm configure` writes it, in your home: `ohm.jsonc` (or name another with `--config`). It says which account (`ATPROTO_DID`), which PDS (`ATPROTO_PDS_ORIGIN`) and which website (`SITE_ORIGIN`).
- An app password for that account, in `ATPROTO_APP_PASSWORD` when you start it:

```sh
ATPROTO_APP_PASSWORD=xxxx-xxxx-xxxx-xxxx npx github:haeinej/ohmmmm serve
```

The password stays in the bridge on your computer. The app never sees it, and no answer carries it. When either is missing, the bridge says so when it starts, and the Publish dialog says what to do.

Why it is safe to leave running:

- It answers only your computer. It listens on 127.0.0.1, and it refuses requests that arrive under any other name, so a website cannot trick your browser into reaching it as another server.
- Every request needs a key. Each browser you pair has its own, which opens everything the app does, and each agent has its own, which opens only the tools for agents. Your home keeps only a fingerprint of each (`.ohmmmm/app-keys.json`, `.ohmmmm/agent-keys.json`), so a program that reads your home's files cannot find a key there. A key is shown once, when it is made: keep an agent's in that agent's settings.
- An agent's session answers only the key that opened it, so one agent cannot use another's.
- **Forgetting one browser.** Settings › Vault in the app lists the browsers paired with your home: each by the name it gave itself (such as "Chrome on macOS"), when it was paired and last used, with "This browser" on the one you are using. **Forget** shuts that one out at once and leaves the rest paired; forgetting this browser takes the app back to the sample. In a terminal, `ohmmmm browsers` lists them with an ID, and `ohmmmm browsers forget <id>` forgets one.
- To shut out every key at once, start the bridge with `ohmmmm serve --new-keys`. Every paired browser must then open the new link, and every agent needs a new key.
- If your bridge was set up before agents had keys of their own, the key it had opens only agents' tools from then on: it is kept as the **shared agent key** (`.ohmmmm/agent-key`), so agents you gave it keep working, and the bridge prints a new link to pair the app again. Agents using it name themselves, but none may take the name of an agent that has its own key. Give each agent its own key, then remove the shared one: `ohmmmm agents remove --shared`.
- In a browser, only the app's own pages, and pages served from your computer, may use it.
- Nothing it writes is lost. Every change goes into the vault's journal, and the vault keeps its versions.

Options: `--port 8766` picks another port. `--pair` prints a link for one more browser as it starts (`ohmmmm pair [--port] [--app]` does the same while it runs). `--new-keys` forgets every paired browser and every agent's key, the shared one included. `--app <origin>` names the app's address, and can be given more than once; give `http://localhost:5190` to pair a development copy of the app. `--agent <name>` names every session an agent's own key does not (the shared agent key's, and the app's), instead of each naming itself. `--config <path>` names the site configuration Publish uses, instead of `ohm.jsonc` in your home.

## For agents (the contract)

Code: [home/serve.mjs](../home/serve.mjs). Tests: [test/serve.test.mjs](../test/serve.test.mjs) (bridge), [app/test/bridge.test.ts](../app/test/bridge.test.ts) (pairing and the media cache). Commands: `ohmmmm serve [--port 8766] [--app <origin>]… [--agent <name>] [--config <path>] [--pair] [--new-keys]` (`--new-token` is the old name of `--new-keys`) `ohmmmm pair [--port 8766] [--app <origin>]…`, `ohmmmm agents [list] | add <name> [--new-key] [--port] | remove <name> | remove --shared` and `ohmmmm browsers [list] | forget <id>`, routed by [bin/ohmmmm.mjs](../bin/ohmmmm.mjs) to home/serve.mjs with the command name first. It needs a home (`findHome`, or `OHMMMM_HOME`) and `node:sqlite` (it re-runs itself with `--experimental-sqlite` when that is missing).

**Every request,** in this order:
1. The `Host` header's hostname must be `127.0.0.1`, `localhost` or `[::1]`, or the answer is 403. The server binds `127.0.0.1` only. There is no option to bind elsewhere.
2. An `Origin` header must be one of `--app` (default `https://app.ohmmmm.com`), or have a loopback hostname, or the answer is 403. An allowed origin gets `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: <origin>`, `Vary: Origin` and `Access-Control-Expose-Headers: Mcp-Session-Id`.
3. `OPTIONS` is the preflight. It is answered 204 without a key, with `Allow-Methods: GET, POST, PUT, DELETE`, `Allow-Headers: Authorization, Content-Type, Accept, Mcp-Session-Id, MCP-Protocol-Version`, `Max-Age: 600`, and `Access-Control-Allow-Private-Network: true` when the request asks for it (Private Network Access).
4. `Authorization: Bearer <key>`: the key's SHA-256 is compared in constant time with every key's (`homeKeys(home)`, read on each request, so a browser paired with `ohmmmm pair` is let in, and a replaced key shut out, without a restart). Without one, or with any other value, the answer is 401 `WWW-Authenticate: Bearer`.
5. An agent's key (its own or the shared one) reaches only `AGENT_ROUTES`: `/vault/mcp`, `/cortex/mcp` and `/`. Anything else is 403 "This key is an agent's…". An app key reaches every route, MCP included. `GET /` answers `you`: `{ app: true }`, `{ agent: <name> }`, or `{ agent: null, shared: true }`.
6. **Sessions.** `httpTransport` is given `open(req)` and `owner(req)`. The bridge keeps who signed each request (a `WeakMap` from the request to `{ kind, name?, shared?, reserved, owner }`, `owner` being the key's SHA-256): a session is opened knowing it, and answers only requests signed with the same key (any other is 404, "no such session"). A session opened with an agent's own key is that agent: `by: agent:<name>` in the vault, `actor` in the cortex, whatever its `initialize` says. A session opened with the shared key or an app key is named by its `initialize` (`clientInfo.name`, made safe) or `--agent`; with the shared key, a name an agent's own key holds, in any letter case, is refused with JSON-RPC error -32001 "<name> has a key of its own…", and no session is made.

**The keys** (all in `<home>/.ohmmmm/`, folder 0700, files 0600, each written whole through a temporary file and a rename):
- `addAgent(home, name, { replace })` → a new `ohm_agent_…` key (32 random bytes in base64url), returned once and never written: `agent-keys.json` holds `{ agents: [{ name, hash: <SHA-256 hex>, made }] }`. A name is `[A-Za-z0-9._-]{1,64}`; a name with a key is refused without `replace` (which shuts the old key out), and so is one that differs from another only in letter case. `agentKeys(home)` lists them; `removeAgent(home, name)` → whether it had one.
- `pairApp(home, { replace, app })` → a new `ohm_app_…` key, returned once and never written: `app-keys.json` gains `{ hash, made, app? }`; `replace` forgets the rest. `appKeys(home)` lists them, each also with `name?` and `seen?`.
- **Browsers.** `browserId(hash)` is the hash's first 12 hex characters. `pairedBrowsers(home)` → `[{ id, made, app?, name?, seen? }]`. `forgetBrowser(home, id)` → whether one had that ID (exactly 12 hex characters). `nameBrowser(home, hash, name)` sets `name` (one line, control characters dropped, at most 80 characters). `browserSeen(home, hash)` sets `seen` at most once an hour; the bridge calls it on an app key's request at most once an hour per key, in memory.
- **The lock.** Every change to `app-keys.json` or `agent-keys.json` (pairing, naming, seen, forgetting, adding or removing an agent, `--new-keys`) reads and writes under `withKeysLock(home, work)`: `.ohmmmm/keys.lock`, made with `wx`, waited for in 25 ms steps up to 10 seconds, and taken over when older than 30 seconds. So the bridge and the commands, in other processes, never write over each other's change.
- `sharedAgentKey(home)` → `{ key, fromOldToken }` or null: `agent-key`, kept as it is, from before agents had their own. It is never made anew; `serve-token` (the one key of the first bridges) becomes it and that file is removed. `removeSharedAgentKey(home)` removes both files.
- `homeKeys(home)` → `who(key)`, with `who.reserved`: a `Map` from each own-key agent name, in lower case, to the name.
- `ohmmmm serve` prints a pairing link when no browser is paired, with `--pair`, or with `--new-keys` (which forgets every browser and every agent's key, the shared one included). Otherwise it says how many browsers are paired and to run `ohmmmm pair`. It names the agents with keys, says to run `ohmmmm agents add <name>`, and names the shared key's file while it is kept. It prints no agent's key. `ohmmmm agents add` prints the key once, with the endpoints and the header to give it.
- `pairingLink(app, url, key)` is `<app>/#bridge=<url>&token=<key>`, URI-encoded (the fragment's name is unchanged, for paired apps).

Writes nothing |

It journals `changed`/`added` `via: publish` (`layer`, `was` on the block; `before`, `hash`), then `published` (`key`, `address`, `record`: the PDS's version, `hash`). When the plan was for a new post and the PDS already holds one under the key with the same fields and files (dates aside), nothing is written to the PDS: the remaining file steps are finished, `published` is journaled only if it is not yet, and the answer is the first try's (`update: false`; `files` the block and its channel as they are) |

Journals `unpublished` (`key`, `address`) and `changed` `via: publish`. Answers `{ path, text, version, layer, removed, address? }`; a public block with no record is only moved |
| `GET /` | `{ home, vault: { scanned }, cortex, endpoints, publishing: { config, password } }` (booleans; the password is never answered) |

Anything else is 404. A `VaultProblem` (such as a collection already running) is 409, a PDS that does not answer is 502 on the publish routes, and anything unexpected is 500 with no detail.

**Publishing** ([vault/vault/publish-block.mjs](../vault/vault/publish-block.mjs), tests in [test/publish.test.mjs](../test/publish.test.mjs) against a PDS on loopback). `blockPost({ files, paths, path, read, label, sublabel, held, key, addresses, did, site, collection })` makes the record from the block alone:
- The record's key is the block's `id` (a TID), so renaming or moving the block never moves its record. A block of kind `post` is refused: post files publish by syncing with the vault service.
- Images: `![[file.ext]]` and `![words](file.ext)` outside code, and `![[a media block]]` (its own file, its `alt`), become `![alt](media:<id>)`, the ID made from the file's name. A file is found from the naming file's folder, then the top of the vault, then by its name when only one file has it. An image from the web, or a file not in the vault, is a `problem`. Bytes are read through `read`, at most 60 MB each, with `imageSize` for width and height.
- An embedded block that is not an image becomes a link to it (a `note`). Links (`recordBody`) go to the post of a block that is public and has a journaled address (`publishedAddresses`: the last `published` per key, unless `unpublished` came after), and become plain words otherwise (a `note`).
- The label is the one given, or `defaultLabel`: the channel's `site.label`/`site.sublabel`, else its title or folder name; none at the top of the vault (`other`). `labelSaved` says the channel already holds it.
- `held` (the record already published under the key) makes it an update: its `slug`, `label`, `sublabel`, `publishedAt` and `canonicalUrl` are kept, and `vault.save` sets `updatedAt`.
- `assembleRecord` and `bodyFacts` make and check the record. `confirm` is `recordVersion({ update, record without publishedAt, files: [[id, cid]] })`.
- Journal: `published` and `unpublished` are `POST_OPS` (vault/vault/journal.mjs), which need a `key`; the scan reads past them, and `via: publish` is one of the vias whose `changed` the scan does not journal again.
- Agents: no local tool or WebMCP tool publishes or takes down. `withVisibility` (agents' requests, the visibility route) still refuses public; only the publish routes use `publishedLayer`.
- Agents hold their own keys, which the bridge refuses on every publish route (and every other route of the person's). A program on this computer can still read what the person's own account can, such as a browser's stored data; the keys keep agents to their tools, not apart from the person's account.

**The app** ([app/src/data/bridge.ts](../app/src/data/bridge.ts)): `pairFromLocation()` reads `#bridge=&token=` at start, and again on `hashchange` (a link pasted into a tab already on the app), reloading when it paired, keeps `{ url, token }` in `localStorage['ohm-bridge']` only for an `http:` loopback origin and a token of 20–200 base64url characters, and clears the fragment with `history.replaceState` whatever it held. `bridgeFetch` signs requests. [app/src/data/browsers.ts](../app/src/data/browsers.ts): `homeKeys()`, `forgetPairedBrowser(id)`, `browserName(ua)` ("Chrome on macOS") and `nameThisBrowser()`, which main.tsx calls once the home opens, and which names this browser only when it has no name yet. Settings › Vault (`PairedBrowsers` in src/workspace/Settings.tsx) lists them, and Forget asks first in a dialog; forgetting this browser calls `forgetBridge()` and goes back to the sample. No tool does either. `createLocalSource` turns a 401 or 403 on `/vault/files` into `NotAllowedError` with `UNPAIRED` ("run ohmmmm pair…"), which main.tsx shows while staying on the sample. `setMediaRemote(bridgeMedia)` makes the home folder the media cache's last source for an original (`src/media/cache.ts`), and bytes are kept only when they hash to the CID asked for.

**The local source** ([app/src/data/local-source.ts](../app/src/data/local-source.ts), tests in [app/test/local-source.test.ts](../app/test/local-source.test.ts)). `createLocalSource(bridge, { onProblem })` reads `/vault/files` once, and returns a `Workspace` (`HomeSource<"sync">` with the workspace's own part, as the sample is), so the store edits it at once:
- A block's ID is its `id`, or `fileId(path)` (`file-` and a hash of the path it was read at, with no `/` or `:`, which the tree's row IDs use) for a file without one. Its layer is its `visibility` (private by default). App-side versions are `local-<n>`; the bridge's versions are kept apart for `If-Match`.
- `saveBlock` merges with `mergeFile(onDisk, path, written, layer)`: the body is replaced; of the properties, only `title`, `channels`, `updated`, `connect`, `icon`, `connectionNotes`, `alt`, `hash`, `mime`, `width` and `height` are set, and only when they differ (an empty list or text counts as none); `id` only where the file has none; `kind` and `visibility` only when the file would not already read as them. Every other property, their order and the file's name stay.
- Writes go back one at a time, in order, each over the version the last one left. A refusal (such as 412) stops writes to that file and calls `onProblem`; `unsent()` says whether anything is not on disk yet (main.tsx asks before the page is left). A new media block is sent as `PUT /media/<cid>` with its `channel`, `slug`, `id`, `title`, `alt`, `name`, `width` and `height`; the block text the bridge wrote is kept, and later edits merge into it.
- `saveChannels` writes each channel's `order` into its `_channel.md` (merged), makes `_channel.md` with an `id` for a new channel, and removes it for a channel deleted when empty.
- **Moves.** A block whose home channel changes is moved in the working copy at once (same file name, in the new folder), and sent as `POST /vault/rename { block, home }`. A channel is moved by `moveChannel(from, to)` (`Workspace.moveChannel`, which the store's `moveChannel` and `renameChannel` call instead of moving each block), sent as `POST /vault/rename { channel, to }`. The answer's `files` settle the working copy: each file's text and version are taken, with any change made meanwhile merged onto them, the blocks are read again, and `onChange` tells the store (`sourceChanged`). Files the rename `left` are named in a notice. An existing file or channel at the destination is refused before anything moves.
- **History.** `createLocalSource` also reads `/vault/journal`, and `pastChanges` turns it into history lines (`createJournal(…, { baseline: true, past })`): each change on the block it is now (its `id`, or its file followed through later moves), by `you` (`by: me`), the agent named, or `outside ohm` (`unknown`). A change whose version was never kept is left out. A change made now is compared with the block as it was read, and never joins a line from the journal.
- **Visibility.** `markVisibility(ref, layer, replaces, why?)` (the store's `setVisibility`, after the control's approval) changes only the layer in the working copy (`mergeFile(onDisk, path, onDisk, layer)`) and sends `POST /vault/visibility`; when an agent is acting (`journal.actor()`, as `withAgent` sets it for `visibility.move`), it sends `asked` and the agent's `why`. Public, to or from, calls `onProblem`. `requests()` and `decide(id, approve)` serve `AgentRequests` (src/workspace/AgentRequests.tsx). `visibility.preview` and `visibility.move` (src/workspace/VisibilityMarks.tsx) are registered while the visibility control is on screen; `visibility.move` is consequential and every move waits for the dialog.
- **Publishing.** `planPublish(ref, label?)`, `publishBlock(ref, draft)` and `unpublishBlock(ref, to)` first wait for every write before them (`onDisk`; a file with a refused write is refused), then call the routes above. `publishBlock` sends the draft's `confirm`, `label` and `sublabel` over the version the draft was planned at, refusing locally when the file has moved on; the answer's `files` settle the working copy. `PublishDialog` and `TakeDownDialog` (src/workspace/PublishDialog.tsx) are opened by the visibility control (src/workspace/VisibilityMarks.tsx) on the home on this computer: Everyone (on a public block, an update), and Only you or Friends on a public block. A new label is planned again 350 ms after typing stops, and Publish waits until the plan on screen matches the fields. No tool is registered for either.
- Not built: `moveVisibility` (between the service's stores), friends, shared reads and the cortex refuse.
- `src/main.tsx` opens the local source at start when a bridge is paired, and stays on the sample with a notice when it does not answer. Settings › Vault shows the home and **Use the sample** (`forgetBridge`).

Sizes are not served; the app makes them.
